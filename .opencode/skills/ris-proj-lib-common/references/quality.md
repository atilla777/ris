# Quality and verification

Use before choosing checks, applying completion criteria, or making a final
assessment. Assess the calling skill's contract, the task's acceptance
criteria, and applicable project requirements together; no one replaces the
others. Determine which checks fit the actual result, rather than imposing
tests meant for another kind of change.

Assess requirements/design basis under common's `published-inputs.md`: direct
acceptance, proven inheritance or a mixture. Check actual derivation and original
source applicability, not merely noncontradiction. Missing a new human decision
is not a defect for wholly inherited content; unsupported material choices are.
This assessment does not let a reviewer accept new behavior for the person.
Also recognize common's scoped prepare policy basis: check exact content/version,
flow authority, historical checked config/default and explicit scope overrides,
not a nonexistent human approval or inheritance proof. Material uncertainty still
needs human resolution; independent review/check/publication gates do not change.

Use the least costly method that supplies sufficient evidence for the specific
claim. Distinguish mandatory, conditionally activated and exploratory checks;
possible experiments are not automatically a required matrix. For ordinary
instruction edits, use reading, affected static checks and required independent
review; no new live run or repeated question about one is needed for source
readiness. Check names/resources/dependencies/parity statically and executable
programs with necessary ordinary tests. Apply the actual target project's rules,
including any prohibition of agent tests. Agent-behavior, reliability, cost and
repeatability claims need applicable existing observations, not static review.
Preserve their versions and limits and historical unmet obligations as unverified;
do not make them an automatic gate for ordinary instruction work. An explicitly
accepted behavioral objective requires separately agreed scope, without bypassing
project prohibitions. Before substantial new verification setup, identify the
uncovered claim, why current evidence or cheaper checks cannot cover it and which
decision it informs. A confirmed contract/platform conflict or materially
growing verification effort returns promptly to the caller/user before dependent
experiments expand. Continue independently useful authorized work. Mandatory
checks require an accepted revision to reduce scope, with unrun claims and limits
explicit; lower cost alone never turns missing evidence into a pass.

For each material completion claim, identify the criterion, suitable check,
observed result, and limit of that evidence. Structure and file presence do
not prove behavior. Use evidence for the current version: after changing an
affected part, reconsider earlier results. Avoid repeating an expensive check
when suitable current evidence already exists. Distinguish checks that passed,
failed, were not run, or genuinely do not apply, with a reason for the latter;
unavailable tools do not make a required check inapplicable.

Do not treat loading a dependency as using it, a small change as exempt from
mandatory checks, a glossary entry as preserving all requirements, or a
rewritten expectation as proof a defect was fixed. Do not bypass a mandatory
storage mechanism when its tool is unavailable. A legitimate scope change,
draft-only request, or actual limitation may alter the achievable result;
report it honestly without retroactively claiming old criteria were met.

If a check fails or behavior diverges from agreed requirements, investigate
the affected part before building on it. If required evidence is absent, mark
that portion unverified; if a critical input, permission, or dependency is
missing, block only the dependent action and continue independent work where
possible. Verify the result within the current task when appropriate; neither
verification nor review automatically starts another lifecycle stage.

Fast (`ris-proj-flow-execute-simple-task`, explicitly or naturally selected) owns
its bounded admission and independent-review applicability: explicit human request,
mandatory applicable target-project policy or concretely explained material risk.
This shared library does not classify work or start review itself. General RIS
independent-review wording does not silently cancel that narrow exception; actual
mandatory Fast policy still applies. Self-check, mandatory checks/TDD and known
adverse findings remain. Inactive review needs no role/format/skip report or fictional
approval; changed subjects get affected self-check and renewed applicability/risk
assessment. Required review needs real independence and applicable reassessment;
unavailable required review blocks dependent gates. Full/feature/bug/direct review
and ordinary publisher obligations remain unchanged outside eligible Fast.

When a project operation relies on a saved change-review gate, read/apply the
installed `ris-proj-op-review-task/references/report-format.md`, the single owner
of format/history and external approval predicates. Do not copy its schema here
or infer pass from a verdict/name. This condition does not impose RIS format on
portable assessments or override explicit fast session-only review. Integrated
feature verification remains a different result, not a change-review report.
